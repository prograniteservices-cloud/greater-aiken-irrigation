# Field Log: Ghost Watering at the Horse Farm

## Site Investigation
Out here in Windsor, we deal with more than just grass; we're keeping pastures and paddock perimeters green for some fine equine athletes. This particular job in Rural Estates involved a massive property running off a deep well. The owner was seeing "phantom watering"—zones staying on long after the controller clicked off, or worse, waking up to a swamp in the middle of a drought. After 40 years in this dirt, I knew it wasn't a computer glitch. High iron content in our local groundwater acts like liquid sandpaper on valve diaphragms, eventually scoring the seat until it can't seal tight.

## Engineering Solution
We didn't just swap the guts and call it a day. We pulled the old, fouled valves and upgraded the manifold with industrial-grade scrubbers designed to handle the grit from Windsor's deep-well systems. To prevent the hammer effect that often rattles these long lateral lines, we integrated **SAM (Seal-A-Matic) check valves** into the heads at the low points. This keeps the lines charged and prevents that nasty back-drainage that sucks silt into the valve body. We also installed **flow sensing telemetry** at the main, so if a valve ever hangs open again, the system shuts itself down and pings the owner's phone before the well pump burns out.

## System Certification
The system was pressure-tested to ensure the new valves snap shut with authority. We recalibrated the zones to ensure head-to-head coverage across the large acreage, giving those horses a soft, green landing. Everything is back in working order, and that well pump is finally getting some well-deserved rest.
